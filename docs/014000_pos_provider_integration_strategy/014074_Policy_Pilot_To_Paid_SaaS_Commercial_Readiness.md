# 014074_Policy_Pilot_To_Paid_SaaS_Commercial_Readiness

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the pilot-to-paid SaaS conversion, operational proof, commercial readiness, pricing confidence, support burden review, customer value evidence, store owner decision, conversion risk, and post-conversion control policy for the Yoonsul Wait/Order Handoff documentation project.

The previous document defined pilot learning daily review, weekly consolidation, and next scope decision policy.

This document defines when and how a controlled pilot may become a paid SaaS customer relationship.

This document does not execute billing, create contracts, send invoices, implement subscription systems, or approve real commercial conversion.

It defines pilot-to-paid conversion readiness policy only.

---

## 3. Core Principle

Paid conversion must be based on operational proof, not optimism.

The project must follow this rule:

> A pilot may convert to paid SaaS only when the store can see repeated operational value, critical risks are controlled, support burden is understood, pricing is transparent, and unresolved limitations are explicitly disclosed.

Commercial conversion without operational proof creates churn, dispute, and trust loss.

---

## 4. Paid Conversion Meaning

Paid conversion means:

- pilot customer accepts continued paid use
- SaaS package scope is defined
- support scope is defined
- provider dependencies are disclosed
- unresolved limitations are disclosed
- billing responsibility is clear
- cancellation/downgrade path is clear
- operational evidence supports value
- post-conversion review cadence exists

Paid conversion is not full product maturity.

It is an early customer commitment under defined scope.

---

## 5. Conversion Preconditions

Pilot-to-paid conversion may be considered only when:

1. pilot scope is documented
2. pilot evidence is complete enough
3. no critical unresolved blocker remains
4. payment uncertainty is handled safely
5. KDS duplicate risk is controlled
6. support masking is stable
7. staff can operate the flow
8. store owner sees measurable or observable value
9. support burden is acceptable
10. provider dependency is understood
11. pricing and fee breakdown are transparent
12. customer exit path is defined

No paid conversion should occur from a single successful demo.

## 9. Operational Proof Categories

Operational proof should be collected across:

- customer session stability
- order intent clarity
- payment certainty handling
- KDS handoff safety
- staff usability
- support recoverability
- provider reliability
- fallback usability
- customer trust
- owner/store value
- evidence completeness
- incident trend improvement

Paid conversion should require more than one category of proof.

---

## 10. Minimum Operational Proof

Minimum proof before paid conversion:

| Proof Area | Requirement |
| ---------- | ----------- |
| Payment | no unresolved critical payment risk |
| KDS | no unresolved duplicate ticket risk |
| Support | masked scoped access works |
| Staff | staff can operate flow without constant panic |
| Customer | customer communication is safe |
| Evidence | pilot evidence packet exists |
| Provider | provider limitations are known |
| Fallback | fallback path works |
| Owner | store owner sees value |
| Scope | limitations are documented |

Minimum proof protects early trust.

---

## 11. SaaS Value Evidence

SaaS value evidence may include:

- reduced staff confusion over time
- faster order intake
- fewer missed orders
- clearer payment recovery
- safer KDS handoff
- better incident traceability
- owner visibility improvement
- support burden measurable
- fallback process improved
- customer experience improved
- pilot issues converted into fixes
- store willingness to continue

Value evidence may be quantitative or qualitative early.

But it must be specific.

---

## 12. Quantitative Evidence Candidates

Possible quantitative evidence:

- number of sessions
- order intent count
- accepted order count
- payment uncertainty rate
- payment recovery time
- KDS handoff success count
- duplicate ticket prevented count
- support case count
- fallback count
- customer recovery count
- staff intervention count
- pilot pause count
- incident count by severity
- repeat issue count
- owner review score

Early numbers may be small.

Trends matter.

---

## 13. Qualitative Evidence Candidates

Possible qualitative evidence:

- owner says visibility improved
- staff says fallback is clearer
- kitchen says ticket confusion reduced
- support says evidence is easier to review
- customer wording reduces anxiety
- provider issue is easier to isolate
- payment uncertainty is less chaotic
- manual recovery is more disciplined
- store can explain what happened after incident

Qualitative evidence must be tied to examples.

---

## 14. Store Owner Value Review

Store owner value review should answer:

- what problem did the system reduce?
- what remained frustrating?
- what would owner pay for?
- what does owner still not trust?
- what support level is expected?
- what provider limitation is acceptable?
- what feature is required before paid use?
- what feature can wait?
- what price feels fair for current scope?
- what would cause cancellation?

Owner value review is central to paid conversion.

---

## 15. Staff Value Review

Staff value review should answer:

- did staff understand states?
- did system reduce or increase work?
- did payment uncertainty handling help?
- did KDS handoff help?
- did Mini Kiosk reduce friction?
- did support escalation help?
- was fallback usable?
- what training is still needed?
- what screen or wording caused confusion?
- would staff accept continued use?

Staff acceptance affects retention.

---

## 16. Customer Trust Review

Customer trust review should answer:

- were customers misled?
- did customers understand delay or uncertainty?
- did customer recovery work?
- did Mini Kiosk feel helpful?
- did staff explanation feel calm?
- did payment concern create anxiety?
- did customer need repeated help?
- did any customer refuse use after issue?

Customer trust is more important than automation speed.

---

## 17. Support Burden Review

Support burden review should answer:

- how many support cases occurred?
- what types of cases occurred?
- were support cases avoidable?
- did support have enough evidence?
- did support access remain masked?
- did support response time meet expectation?
- did support burden exceed package level?
- does paid package need higher support tier?
- what support tasks should become SOP or UI improvement?

Paid conversion must price support burden honestly.

---

## 18. Provider Dependency Review

Provider dependency review should answer:

- which provider path was used?
- was Toss path stable enough?
- was OKPOS compatibility still assumption or verified?
- was PAYCO involved?
- were provider callbacks reliable?
- were provider failures understandable?
- were provider limitations disclosed?
- does paid scope depend on provider approval?
- is provider disable path available?
- is provider support/dealer dependency clear?

Provider uncertainty must be disclosed before conversion.

---

## 19. Payment Safety Review

Payment safety review should confirm:

- no unresolved duplicate charge risk
- payment uncertainty display works
- refund/cancel boundary is clear
- support cannot mutate payment truth
- invalid provider callback is rejected
- replay is blocked
- payment evidence exists
- customer recovery path is defined
- payment limitation is disclosed

Payment weakness can block conversion.

---

## 20. KDS Safety Review

KDS safety review should confirm:

- no unresolved duplicate ticket risk
- unsafe handoff is blocked
- held ticket is not prepared
- cancellation impact is reviewed
- degraded kitchen note path works
- KDS evidence exists
- kitchen staff understands states
- KDS limitation is disclosed

KDS weakness can block conversion if kitchen operation depends on it.

---

## 21. Mini Kiosk Readiness Review

Mini Kiosk readiness review should confirm:

- customer session flow is usable
- order intent capture is clear
- timeout handling works
- unsupported paths are hidden
- payment state display is honest
- provider unavailable state is clear
- support handoff is available
- staff can supervise or recover
- customer confusion is acceptable

Mini Kiosk must not overpromise.

---

## 22. Open Blocker Review

Before conversion, review:

- critical blockers
- high blockers
- recurring medium issues
- waived issues
- deferred issues
- scope restrictions
- unresolved provider assumptions
- unresolved support burden
- unresolved training gaps

Paid conversion should not hide open issues.

---

## 23. Conversion Blockers

Conversion should be blocked if:

- critical payment blocker remains
- duplicate KDS ticket risk remains
- support masking failure remains
- tenant/store leakage risk remains
- provider path required for paid use is unverified
- rollback/disable path fails
- staff cannot operate without constant intervention
- evidence cannot prove safety
- owner does not understand limitations
- pricing hides external fees
- cancellation path is unclear

Blocking conversion protects long-term trust.

## 25. Paid Pilot Extension

Paid pilot extension may apply when:

- value exists
- some blockers remain
- scope is limited
- support is still active
- owner is willing to pay for continued controlled use
- standard subscription is premature

Paid pilot extension should have:

- duration
- price
- scope
- support level
- limitations
- next review date
- exit path

---

## 26. Early SaaS Subscription

Early SaaS subscription may apply when:

- core flow is stable within agreed scope
- owner sees repeatable value
- support burden is understood
- critical blockers are resolved
- pricing is transparent
- cancellation/downgrade path is defined
- provider limitations are disclosed

Early subscription is not full mature SaaS.

It is controlled commercial operation.

---

## 27. Limited Scope Subscription

Limited scope subscription may apply when:

- only part of the system is stable
- customer wants that part
- unstable modules remain disabled
- billing reflects limited scope
- limitations are disclosed
- support scope is clear

Example:

    Mini Kiosk intent capture only
    Provider gateway evidence only
    KDS handoff disabled
    Payment recovery support only

Do not charge for unsafe modules.

---

## 28. Package Candidate Mapping

Conversion should map to package candidate.

Possible package candidates:

- Store OS Basic
- Store OS Plus
- Mini Kiosk Module
- KDS Handoff Module
- Provider Gateway Module
- Support Operations Add-On
- Pilot Evidence Add-On
- Early SaaS Bundle
- Franchise OS Deferred Signal

Final pricing may be decided later.

Package must match actual usable scope.

---

## 29. Fee Transparency Rule

Paid conversion proposal must separate:

- Yoonsul SaaS fee
- provider fee
- payment provider fee
- hardware fee
- support fee
- setup/onboarding fee
- optional add-on fee
- discount or pilot credit
- tax or external charges if applicable

Do not hide provider or hardware costs inside vague package language.

---

## 30. Discount And Pilot Credit Rule

Pilot discount or credit should define:

- reason
- amount or percentage
- duration
- expiration
- standard price reference
- renewal condition
- scope limitation
- whether it applies to SaaS only or includes support/hardware
- no automatic permanent discount

Discount must not hide weak value.

---

## 31. Limitations Disclosure Rule

Conversion proposal should disclose:

- supported provider path
- unsupported provider paths
- payment limitations
- KDS limitations
- Mini Kiosk limitations
- support hours or scope
- manual fallback reliance
- open waivers
- deferred features
- known risks
- cancellation path

A paid customer should not discover limitations after payment.

## 35. Post-Conversion Monitoring

After paid conversion, monitor:

- payment uncertainty
- KDS issues
- support cases
- fallback frequency
- staff confusion
- customer complaints
- provider instability
- evidence completeness
- owner satisfaction
- churn risk
- upgrade signal
- downgrade signal

Paid conversion begins retention work.

It does not end pilot learning.

---

## 36. Post-Conversion Review Cadence

Recommended cadence:

| Review | Cadence |
| ------ | ------- |
| First week review | weekly |
| First month review | weekly or biweekly |
| Support burden review | weekly |
| Payment/KDS safety review | weekly |
| Owner value review | monthly |
| Renewal risk review | before billing renewal |
| Package fit review | monthly during early stage |

Cadence may reduce as stability improves.

---

## 37. Early Churn Risk Signals

Early churn signals:

- owner says value unclear
- staff avoids using system
- support burden feels high
- payment trust issue repeats
- KDS confusion repeats
- provider instability continues
- price feels unfair
- limitations were misunderstood
- fallback used too often
- customer complaints increase
- evidence is not reviewed

Early churn must trigger intervention.

## 40. Anti-Patterns

The following are prohibited:

- converting to paid after one successful demo
- hiding unresolved blockers
- charging for unsafe module
- hiding provider fees
- hiding hardware cost
- hiding support burden
- presenting pilot as mature production
- offering permanent discount without standard price
- selling broad provider compatibility not proven
- selling KDS automation with duplicate risk unresolved
- selling payment flow with uncertainty hidden
- ignoring staff resistance
- ignoring customer trust risk
- treating paid conversion as end of learning

## 43. Conclusion

Pilot-to-paid conversion should happen only when operational evidence supports continued paid use.

The safe conversion flow is:

    pilot evidence
        -> daily and weekly learning review
        -> operational value proof
        -> blocker and waiver review
        -> support burden review
        -> provider/payment/KDS safety review
        -> transparent fee proposal
        -> limitation disclosure
        -> paid conversion decision
        -> post-conversion monitoring

Paid conversion is not the end of validation.

It is the start of accountable SaaS operation under a clearly defined scope.

This document protects customer trust, pricing credibility, SaaS retention, and future franchise scalability.
