04220 Kitchen Display Staff Role And Training SOP

1\. Purpose

This SOP defines the staff roles, operating responsibilities, and training requirements for using the kitchen display system during normal, delayed, exception, and degraded kitchen operation.

The purpose is to ensure that KDS improves kitchen continuity without creating confusion about transaction authority, refund authority, menu authority, or staff accountability.

KDS is a kitchen execution tool.

KDS is not POS truth.

KDS is not payment authority.

KDS is not refund authority.

2\. Scope

This SOP applies to:

\- Kitchen staff using KDS
\- Front staff coordinating with kitchen
\- Store manager or shift lead reviewing exceptions
\- Staff training for KDS ticket states
\- Staff training for manual fallback
\- Staff training for allergy/caution visibility
\- Staff training for delay, retry, remake, and duplicate suspected states
\- Staff training for sold-out and menu availability conflicts
\- Staff training for customer-safe communication

This SOP does not define:

\- POS implementation
\- KDS vendor configuration
\- Printer protocol
\- Payroll policy
\- Staff disciplinary process
\- Refund policy
\- Customer compensation formula
\- Inventory deduction
\- Menu master management
\- AI prediction behavior

3\. Core Principle

Staff must understand the difference between order truth, kitchen work, fallback evidence, and customer recovery.

POS accepted order \= commercial truth
KDS ticket \= kitchen execution projection
Manual kitchen note \= fallback evidence
Manager review \= exception resolution path
Customer recovery \= service resolution workflow

A staff member may act on kitchen work.

A staff member must not infer payment, refund, or final customer recovery authority from KDS state alone.

KDS operation may involve the following role families.

Kitchen Staff

5\. Kitchen Staff Responsibilities

Kitchen staff are responsible for preparing assigned KDS tickets correctly and safely.

Kitchen staff should:

\- Read ticket item and quantity
\- Check modifiers
\- Check allergy/caution notes
\- Follow station route
\- Mark progress only when appropriate
\- Mark delay when the station cannot proceed
\- Request help when ticket is unclear
\- Avoid duplicate preparation
\- Preserve manual notes during fallback
\- Escalate allergy/caution uncertainty
\- Escalate sold-out conflicts after acceptance

Kitchen staff must not:

\- Delete accepted work silently
\- Ignore modifiers
\- Ignore allergy/caution notes
\- Treat KDS completion as payment completion
\- Approve refunds from kitchen display
\- Prepare duplicate tickets without review
\- Hide mistakes by clearing tickets
\- Treat dismissed alert as resolved

6\. Station Lead Responsibilities

The station lead coordinates a specific preparation area.

Station lead should:

\- Monitor station ticket queue
\- Confirm station delay
\- Control station duplicate risk
\- Reassign station work when allowed
\- Confirm station completion
\- Preserve station-specific manual notes
\- Escalate unresolved station conflicts
\- Train station staff on KDS state meaning

Station lead must not:

\- Override POS order truth
\- Approve refund
\- Disable menu master across store without authority
\- Hide station overload
\- Resolve allergy/caution uncertainty casually

7\. Kitchen Lead Responsibilities

Kitchen lead oversees kitchen execution across stations.

Kitchen lead should:

\- Monitor overall kitchen flow
\- Resolve station conflicts
\- Confirm kitchen delay severity
\- Approve ordinary kitchen quality remake where policy allows
\- Escalate customer recovery remake if manager approval is required
\- Enter or recommend degraded operation when KDS cannot be trusted
\- Confirm duplicate suspected tickets
\- Confirm manual kitchen queue during fallback
\- Support post-recovery reconciliation

Kitchen lead must not:

\- Mutate payment
\- Approve refund unless separately authorized outside KDS
\- Delete event history
\- Treat manual note as POS truth
\- Ignore mismatch between POS, KDS, and manual notes

8\. Front Staff Responsibilities

Front staff coordinate between customer-facing flow, POS, and kitchen.

Front staff should:

\- Confirm POS accepted order when kitchen asks
\- Communicate customer timing or table context
\- Relay safe delay messages
\- Confirm pickup or serving readiness
\- Escalate customer dissatisfaction
\- Help preserve order references during fallback
\- Avoid overpromising refund or compensation

Front staff must not:

\- Tell kitchen to prepare unpaid/draft cart as normal order unless store policy allows specific exception
\- Promise refund from KDS delay alone
\- Blame KDS, POS, vendor, or staff to customer
\- Hide customer complaints from manager
\- Remove sold-out conflict without review

9\. Packing / Handoff Staff Responsibilities

Packing or handoff staff ensure prepared items move to the correct customer, table, pickup shelf, or delivery flow.

Packing/handoff staff should:

\- Confirm item and quantity
\- Confirm packaging requirement
\- Check modifiers related to packaging
\- Check allergy/caution labels
\- Confirm order grouping
\- Mark handoff-related readiness where applicable
\- Report missing station items
\- Report stale ready items
\- Report duplicate packing risk

Packing/handoff staff must not:

\- Treat kitchen ready as customer received
\- Merge allergy/caution items without clear marking
\- Hand off uncertain items without confirmation
\- Clear tickets to reduce screen clutter without completing real handoff

10\. Shift Lead / Store Manager Responsibilities

The shift lead or store manager owns exception coordination during service.

Manager should:

\- Declare degraded operation when needed
\- Assign fallback responsibilities
\- Resolve POS/KDS/manual mismatch
\- Approve manager-required remake
\- Review sold-out after acceptance
\- Review cancellation after kitchen start
\- Review duplicate suspected tickets
\- Review customer-impacting delay
\- Confirm recovery completion
\- Preserve evidence
\- Support staff training

Manager must not:

\- Rewrite history to hide failure
\- Treat metrics as automatic blame
\- Close degraded operation without reconciliation
\- Approve refund solely from KDS state without refund policy
\- Ignore repeated KDS reliability issues

11\. HQ Support Responsibilities

HQ support may review policy, reliability patterns, vendor issues, and training gaps.

HQ support may:

\- Analyze KDS reliability
\- Review repeated degraded operation
\- Improve SOP
\- Review audit evidence
\- Support vendor escalation
\- Update training material
\- Define policy guardrails

HQ support must not:

\- Silently mutate live store order truth
\- Directly override store kitchen flow without defined authority
\- Use KDS metrics as automatic staff punishment
\- Treat remote visibility as operational resolution

12\. Vendor Support Boundary

Vendor support may assist with technical KDS issues.

Vendor support must not be treated as authority for:

\- POS order truth
\- Payment state
\- Refund decision
\- Customer recovery
\- Menu master
\- Inventory master
\- Staff accountability

Vendor support may help explain or repair technical delivery, display, routing, or device issues.

14\. KDS Basic Vocabulary Training

Staff must understand the following basic vocabulary.

Term| Staff Meaning
POS accepted order| Order accepted by transaction-authoritative system
KDS ticket| Kitchen work projection
Station route| Where the ticket should be prepared
Modifier| Preparation option or change
Allergy/caution| Safety-sensitive note
Delay| Kitchen cannot proceed normally
Retry| Ticket delivery or visibility is being attempted again
Remake| Kitchen prepares item again for a reason
Duplicate suspected| Same work may appear more than once
Manual recovery| Normal KDS state cannot be trusted
Sold-out conflict| Accepted work conflicts with availability

15\. Ticket State Training

Staff must be trained on basic KDS ticket states.

Received / Seen
Acknowledged
In Progress
Delayed
Ready
Completed
Retry Required
Remake Required
Duplicate Suspected
Manual Recovery Required
Exception

The exact runtime labels may vary, but the operating meaning must be consistent.

16\. Received / Seen Training

Received or Seen means the ticket reached the display, printer, bridge, or staff awareness layer.

Staff must not assume that received means preparation started.

Staff should confirm whether action is required.

17\. Acknowledged Training

Acknowledged means kitchen staff accepted the ticket into active kitchen awareness.

Staff should acknowledge only when they understand the ticket enough to proceed or queue it responsibly.

If modifiers or allergy notes are unclear, staff should not acknowledge as normal without escalation.

18\. In Progress Training

In Progress means preparation has started or the station has committed to the work.

After In Progress, cancellation requires explicit handling.

Staff must not silently remove in-progress tickets.

19\. Delayed Training

Delayed means the ticket cannot proceed within normal kitchen flow.

Staff should mark or report delay when:

\- Ingredient is not ready
\- Station is overloaded
\- Equipment is unavailable
\- Modifier needs confirmation
\- Allergy/caution needs confirmation
\- Timing is intentionally held
\- KDS or routing issue affects work

Delay does not automatically mean refund or compensation.

20\. Ready Training

Ready means kitchen preparation is complete for the relevant ticket or station segment.

Ready does not necessarily mean:

\- Customer received the item
\- Delivery picked up the item
\- Payment is settled
\- The whole grouped order is complete

Packing or handoff may still be required.

21\. Completed Training

Completed means kitchen execution responsibility is closed for that ticket.

Staff should mark completed only when the kitchen responsibility is actually done.

Completed must not be used to hide stale or confusing tickets.

22\. Retry Training

Retry means ticket delivery, display, or projection may be attempted again.

Staff must watch for duplicate risk.

If a ticket appears after manual preparation started, staff should mark or escalate Duplicate Suspected.

Retry does not mean a new POS order.

23\. Remake Training

Remake means the kitchen prepares an item again.

Staff must record or select a remake reason where possible.

Remake reasons may include:

\- Wrong item
\- Wrong modifier
\- Quality issue
\- Spillage
\- Temperature issue
\- Customer recovery
\- Delivery damage
\- Allergy/caution concern

Remake does not automatically mean refund, compensation, or staff blame.

24\. Duplicate Suspected Training

Duplicate Suspected means the same accepted work may appear more than once.

Staff should not prepare duplicate work unless confirmed by kitchen lead or manager.

Possible duplicate indicators:

\- Same order line appears twice
\- Ticket appears after retry
\- Printer printed twice
\- Manual note already exists
\- Replay happened after reconnect
\- Staff verbally called same item twice

Duplicate suspected must be reviewed.

25\. Manual Recovery Training

Manual Recovery Required means the normal KDS flow cannot be trusted.

Staff must switch to the defined fallback mode.

Staff must preserve:

\- Time
\- Order reference if available
\- Item
\- Quantity
\- Modifier
\- Allergy/caution note
\- Fulfillment type
\- Staff initials
\- Manual state
\- Reason for note

Manual note is evidence, not POS truth.

26\. Modifier Training

Staff must treat modifiers as preparation-critical data.

Examples:

\- No onion
\- Extra sauce
\- Sauce separate
\- Spicy level
\- Protein option
\- Rice amount
\- Packaging option
\- Temperature request

If modifier data is missing or unclear, staff should escalate rather than guess.

27\. Allergy and Caution Training

Allergy and caution notes require higher attention than ordinary preferences.

Training rules:

\- Read allergy/caution before starting prep.
\- Do not merge allergy-sensitive tickets casually.
\- Preserve allergy/caution during manual fallback.
\- Escalate unclear allergy/caution notes.
\- Treat missing allergy display as safety exception.
\- Do not dismiss allergy/caution alert as resolved without action.

Allergy/caution loss is a safety risk.

28\. Sold-out Conflict Training

Sold-out conflict occurs when availability and accepted order state disagree.

Staff should know:

\- Sold-out blocks future orderability where integrated.
\- Sold-out does not silently erase accepted order.
\- If accepted item cannot be prepared, escalate.
\- KDS may display conflict but does not mutate POS truth.
\- Customer recovery or refund decision is outside KDS state alone.
\- Manual note must preserve affected item and time.

29\. Cancellation Race Training

Cancellation race occurs when cancellation and kitchen work overlap.

Examples:

\- Customer cancels after ticket sent
\- POS cancels after kitchen starts
\- KDS is ready while POS shows cancelled
\- Staff started manually before cancellation appeared

Staff should not silently delete the ticket.

Manager review may be needed depending on kitchen progress.

30\. Degraded Operation Training

Staff must know when KDS is degraded.

Signs include:

\- Tickets delayed
\- Screen unavailable
\- Printer duplicated
\- POS and KDS mismatch
\- Ticket state stale
\- Device rebooted
\- Manual order already started
\- Staff cannot trust queue

Training must include the exact fallback method chosen by the store.

32\. Customer-safe Communication Training

Staff should use calm, non-blaming language.

Allowed examples:

Your order is being prepared.
Preparation may take a little longer than expected.
A staff member is checking your item.
This item needs confirmation before preparation continues.
Your order is ready for pickup.

Avoid:

The KDS failed.
The POS made a mistake.
The kitchen lost your ticket.
The staff forgot.
You will automatically get a refund.
The system is broken.

Customer communication must not promise refund or compensation unless policy allows.

33\. Training Levels

Training may be grouped by staff role.

Training Level| Audience| Focus
Level 1| All service staff| Basic ticket reading and safe escalation
Level 2| Kitchen/station staff| State changes, modifiers, station flow
Level 3| Kitchen lead/front lead| Delay, remake, duplicate, fallback
Level 4| Manager| Degraded operation, recovery, evidence
Level 5| HQ/support| Audit, reliability, SOP improvement

35\. Pre-shift KDS Readiness Check

Before service, responsible staff should check:

\[ \] KDS screen/device visible.
\[ \] Printer fallback available if used.
\[ \] Staff know station assignment.
\[ \] Staff know manual fallback location.
\[ \] Manual note sheets or form available.
\[ \] Allergy/caution display is visible.
\[ \] Sold-out/current availability is reviewed.
\[ \] Manager escalation path is clear.
\[ \] Staff know who controls degraded mode.

This does not need to be heavy, but it must be practical.

36\. During-service Conduct

During service, staff should:

\- Keep ticket queue readable
\- Avoid clearing tickets without real action
\- Escalate unclear tickets
\- Preserve safety notes
\- Mark delays honestly
\- Avoid duplicate preparation
\- Use manual notes when needed
\- Communicate calmly
\- Ask manager for recovery decisions

KDS should reduce chaos, not hide it.

37\. Post-service Review

After service, manager or lead may review:

\- Delayed tickets
\- Remake tickets
\- Duplicate suspected cases
\- Manual recovery cases
\- Sold-out conflicts
\- Allergy/caution exceptions
\- POS/KDS mismatch
\- Customer-impacting issues

The goal is improvement.

The goal is not automatic blame.

38\. Prohibited Actions

Staff must not:

\- Treat KDS as payment truth
\- Approve refund from KDS state alone
\- Ignore modifiers
\- Ignore allergy/caution notes
\- Clear tickets to hide delay
\- Prepare duplicate suspected item without review
\- Throw away manual notes before reconciliation
\- Blame system or staff to customer
\- Treat dismissed alert as resolved
\- Treat screen recovery as full recovery
\- Mutate menu availability without authority
\- Delete accepted order work silently

39\. Evidence and Audit

Training must emphasize that evidence matters.

Important evidence may include:

\- Ticket state change
\- Delay reason
\- Remake reason
\- Duplicate suspected note
\- Manual kitchen note
\- Sold-out conflict
\- Allergy/caution issue
\- Cancellation race
\- Manager review
\- Recovery decision

Evidence supports reconstruction and improvement.

Evidence is not automatic staff punishment.
