# 14028_Policy_Early_SaaS_Customer_Success_Retention

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the early SaaS customer success policy, support tier boundary, retention signal, churn risk signal, expansion signal, module adoption review, support burden tracking, and Franchise OS readiness signal for the Yoonsul Wait/Order Handoff project.

The previous document defined pilot-to-paid SaaS conversion and early customer commitment.

This document defines what must happen after a store becomes an early paid SaaS customer.

This document does not operate customer support, implement customer success software, create billing, define final SLA, or launch Franchise OS.

It defines customer success and retention signal policy only.

## 3. Core Principle

Early SaaS retention depends on operational value, not feature count.

The project must follow this rule:

> A store will remain a SaaS customer only if the system clearly reduces operational friction, improves visibility, supports recovery, and justifies the monthly fee.

Many features do not guarantee retention.

Repeated daily value does.

---

## 4. Early SaaS Customer Definition

An early SaaS customer is a store that:

- completed pilot or discovery
- agreed to paid use
- has defined package scope
- has support commitment
- has provider stack recorded
- has data responsibility recorded
- has cancellation path
- has next review date
- may still have early-adopter limitations

Early SaaS customer is not yet necessarily a standard SaaS customer.

---

## 5. Customer Success Objectives

Customer success objectives:

- confirm customer receives recurring value
- prevent early churn
- reduce support confusion
- identify training gaps
- measure module adoption
- identify provider issues
- detect payment/KDS risk
- improve onboarding
- validate pricing
- prepare expansion where appropriate
- gather Franchise OS signals

Customer success is evidence collection plus retention support.

---

## 6. Support Tier Structure

Recommended support tiers:

| Tier | Meaning |
| ---- | ------- |
| Basic Support | documentation and standard inquiry |
| Operations Support | active incident/recovery support |
| Provider Support Coordination | provider/dealer issue triage |
| Premium Operations Support | higher-touch store operation support |
| Franchise Support | HQ/multi-store governance support |

Final tier names and prices are deferred.

---

## 7. Basic Support

Basic Support may include:

- usage guide
- basic troubleshooting
- email/chat inquiry
- standard FAQ
- setup clarification
- module explanation
- billing clarification
- simple report guidance

Basic Support does not include:

- urgent payment incident handling
- custom provider debugging
- on-site support
- full training
- hardware replacement
- dealer coordination
- custom report development
- SLA-backed incident response

Basic Support is suitable for stable, low-complexity stores.

---

## 8. Operations Support

Operations Support may include:

- order handoff issue review
- payment uncertainty support
- KDS issue triage
- Mini Kiosk session issue review
- staff workflow support
- support case tracking
- monthly issue summary
- recovery evidence review
- owner usage review

Operations Support is likely important for early SaaS customers.

It may be bundled or charged separately.

---

## 9. Provider Support Coordination

Provider Support Coordination may include:

- Toss issue classification
- OKPOS/OKDC issue classification
- PAYCO channel issue classification
- dealer contact routing
- provider evidence collection
- provider blocker update
- provider outage impact review
- provider integration health summary

Yoonsul should coordinate provider issues but must not promise control over provider systems unless contractually supported.

---

## 10. Premium Operations Support

Premium Operations Support may include:

- scheduled operations review
- deeper incident analysis
- staff retraining session
- module usage optimization
- dashboard review
- KDS performance review
- payment recovery review
- support burden report
- store improvement recommendation

Premium support should be priced according to actual workload.

---

## 11. Franchise Support

Franchise Support may include:

- HQ dashboard support
- multi-store issue comparison
- SOP/training support
- owner/store escalation
- incident governance
- provider performance summary
- store compliance evidence
- franchise rollout assistance

Franchise Support should only be offered when Franchise OS scope is mature enough.

## 12. Retention Signal Categories

Retention signals should be tracked across categories:

- module usage
- owner value
- staff adoption
- customer flow
- provider reliability
- payment recovery
- KDS usefulness
- Mini Kiosk usefulness
- support burden
- pricing acceptance
- renewal intention
- expansion interest

Retention must be judged by multiple signals, not one metric.

---

## 13. Positive Retention Signals

Positive signals include:

- owner checks dashboard regularly
- staff uses system without reminders
- payment uncertainty is resolved faster
- KDS reduces kitchen confusion
- Mini Kiosk reduces order burden
- support cases decrease over time
- owner asks about additional modules
- store accepts monthly fee without repeated objection
- provider issues are diagnosable
- staff training becomes easier
- store wants second device
- store asks about multi-store support
- store recommends system to another owner

These suggest product-market fit direction.

---

## 14. Churn Risk Signals

Churn risk signals include:

- owner stops checking dashboard
- staff bypasses system
- support incidents repeat without improvement
- payment issues remain unresolved
- KDS creates more confusion than value
- Mini Kiosk has low completion rate
- provider errors are blamed on Yoonsul
- owner questions monthly fee repeatedly
- pricing feels unclear
- staff complains about extra work
- hardware fails often
- data does not match POS
- refund/cancel flow remains confusing
- owner requests cancellation or downgrade

Churn risk should trigger review before cancellation occurs.

---

## 15. Expansion Signals

Expansion signals include:

- store asks for Mini Kiosk add-on
- store asks for KDS add-on
- store asks for analytics
- owner wants additional device
- owner asks about another store
- store wants staff module
- store wants inventory/sold-out linkage
- store wants customer membership linkage
- owner asks about franchise use
- support issue becomes repeatable module opportunity
- provider integration value is clearly recognized

Expansion should still follow scope and readiness gates.

Adoption signals:

Adoption signals:

- prep status updates

- resolution time improves

## 19. Owner Satisfaction Review

Owner satisfaction should be reviewed periodically.

Questions:

1. What value did you notice this week?
2. What was still frustrating?
3. Which feature would you miss if removed?
4. Which feature did you not use?
5. Was monthly fee understandable?
6. Did support meet expectations?
7. Did staff actually use it?
8. Did customers benefit?
9. Did provider/POS issues become clearer?
10. Would you continue next month?
11. Would you recommend this to another store?
12. What would make this worth upgrading?

Owner satisfaction should be recorded as evidence.

---

## 20. Staff Adoption Review

Staff adoption should be reviewed.

Questions:

1. Do staff use the system during peak time?
2. Do staff bypass it?
3. Which step is confusing?
4. Does it reduce or increase work?
5. Do staff understand payment uncertainty?
6. Do staff understand KDS state?
7. Do staff know when to call support?
8. Does Mini Kiosk reduce counter burden?
9. Is training sufficient?
10. What manual workaround appears?

Staff adoption is often more important than owner enthusiasm.

---

## 21. Customer Flow Review

Customer flow should be reviewed.

Check:

- order start rate
- order completion rate
- payment completion rate
- abandonment point
- staff assistance frequency
- customer confusion
- repeated use
- complaint
- positive feedback
- language issue
- accessibility issue
- waiting/table/pickup confusion

Customer flow affects Mini Kiosk retention value.

---

## 22. Provider Reliability Review

Provider reliability should be reviewed.

Check:

- provider event delay
- provider mismatch
- webhook/callback issue
- local daemon issue
- POS state mismatch
- payment lookup issue
- refund/cancel ambiguity
- dealer support delay
- provider outage
- data access limitation
- provider documentation gap

Provider reliability affects Yoonsul support burden.

---

## 23. Payment Recovery Review

Payment recovery must be reviewed carefully.

Check:

- uncertain payment count
- duplicate payment attempt count
- refund/cancel confusion
- resolution time
- customer complaint
- support involvement
- provider involvement
- evidence completeness
- staff understanding
- owner trust

Payment recovery value can be a strong retention driver.

Payment recovery failure can be a strong churn driver.

---

## 24. KDS Value Review

KDS value should be reviewed.

Check:

- kitchen adoption
- missed order reduction
- duplicate ticket reduction
- delay visibility
- prep status reliability
- cancellation handling
- remake/retry handling
- peak-time usefulness
- staff trust
- kitchen complaint

KDS must prove practical kitchen value.

---

## 25. Mini Kiosk Value Review

Mini Kiosk value should be reviewed.

Check:

- staff burden reduction
- customer completion rate
- payment completion rate
- order accuracy
- menu clarity
- device reliability
- customer confusion
- table/waiting/pickup linkage
- support cases
- owner perceived value

Mini Kiosk should be judged by completed recoverable flow, not only screen display.

---

## 26. Retention Review Cadence

Recommended retention review cadence:

| Timing | Review Focus |
| ------ | ------------ |
| First week | onboarding, critical confusion, support burden |
| First month | module usage, owner value, staff adoption |
| Monthly early stage | retention signal, churn risk, support cost |
| After major incident | trust recovery, blocker update |
| Before renewal | value summary, pricing acceptance |
| Before upgrade | expansion readiness |
| Before cancellation | churn reason and exit evidence |

Review cadence may vary by support tier.

## 29. Churn Prevention Actions

Possible churn prevention actions:

- retraining
- support review
- module simplification
- dashboard adjustment
- provider issue escalation
- payment recovery review
- KDS workflow correction
- Mini Kiosk UI guidance
- pricing clarification
- downgrade option
- pause risky module
- hardware replacement review
- owner value review

Do not solve churn only by discount.

Discount without value correction delays churn.

---

## 30. Expansion Readiness Actions

Expansion may proceed when:

- current module usage is healthy
- support burden is manageable
- owner sees value
- staff adoption is adequate
- payment/KDS risk is controlled
- provider stack is stable
- pricing is accepted
- additional module has clear use case

Expansion actions:

- propose add-on
- propose device increase
- propose KDS/Mini Kiosk
- propose analytics
- propose multi-store dashboard
- propose Franchise OS discovery
- create upgrade decision record

Expansion must be controlled.

---

## 31. Franchise OS Signal

Franchise OS signal appears when:

- owner operates multiple stores
- store asks for HQ dashboard
- store wants SOP/training governance
- store wants multi-store comparison
- provider stack needs standardization
- owner wants menu governance
- recurring incident patterns need HQ oversight
- support data becomes cross-store useful
- analytics becomes multi-store relevant

Franchise OS signal does not mean Franchise OS is ready.

It means future linkage should be tracked.

---

## 32. Renewal Decision

Renewal decision should consider:

- value delivered
- support burden
- pricing acceptance
- module adoption
- provider stability
- unresolved blockers
- customer satisfaction
- churn risk
- expansion potential
- strategic value

Renewal outcomes:

- renew same package
- renew with downgrade
- renew with upgrade
- extend pilot
- convert to standard SaaS
- pause subscription
- exit customer
- defer expansion
- prepare Franchise OS discovery

---

## 33. Exit Evidence

If customer exits, record:

- exit reason
- package
- modules used
- provider stack
- support history
- unresolved issues
- pricing concern
- staff adoption issue
- owner value issue
- hardware issue
- provider issue
- data export request
- cancellation date
- lessons learned
- future reactivation possibility

Exit evidence is valuable.

Do not hide churn.

---

## 34. Anti-Patterns

The following are prohibited:

- assuming paid customer is automatically retained
- measuring retention only by payment status
- ignoring low usage
- ignoring staff bypass
- solving all churn risk with discount
- hiding support burden
- blaming provider without evidence
- ignoring owner confusion about value
- upselling before current module is stable
- offering Franchise OS before readiness
- ignoring payment trust risk
- ignoring hardware reliability
- failing to record exit reason
- treating churn as shame instead of learning

## 37. Conclusion

Early SaaS success depends on repeated operational value.

The project must track:

- owner satisfaction
- staff adoption
- module usage
- support burden
- provider reliability
- payment recovery
- KDS value
- Mini Kiosk value
- pricing acceptance
- churn risk
- expansion readiness
- Franchise OS signal

The correct flow is:

    Paid Customer
        -> Support Tier
        -> Health Review
        -> Retention Signal
        -> Churn Prevention / Expansion Decision
        -> Renewal / Upgrade / Downgrade / Exit
        -> Learning Back Into Product And Pricing

This document prepares early SaaS customer success discipline before broader Franchise OS expansion.
